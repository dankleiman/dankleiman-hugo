I care just as much about "how you practice" as "what you practice". At a certain point, you should be paying attention to both equally.

For your own practice, I think Dr. Cobb sums it up nicely [here](/2011/01/08/work-speed-mistakes/), where he describes the delicate balance between putting your head down and working hard and sitting back to reflect on your hard work.

When you think about crafting a training experience for a student or client, you need to think about how you can, in the words of Dr. BJ Fogg, "put hot triggers in the path of motivated people."

On a whole different level, take the advice of [Taoist Sage Liu Hung Chieh](http://www.energyarts.com/content/liu-hung-chieh-taoist-lineage-master): "you become what you practice."
